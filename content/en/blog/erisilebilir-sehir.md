I spent a little over a month in England on a language course at EC
English. Besides learning the language, that time taught me something
else: how much easier daily life becomes when a city is designed with
disabled people in mind.

## Why this matters to me

I use a wheelchair. I experience how accessible a city is directly, every
day. It also played a big part in my decision to study social work. That
is why I share my daily life and the accessibility situations I come
across on Instagram and TikTok.

## On the train: a first

When I took a train in England for the first time, I wanted to see how
accessible public transport is for a wheelchair user. The video I shared
of it has been viewed more than 600,000 times across TikTok and
Instagram. That interest shows that people are curious about
accessibility and want to talk about it.

## On the bus: a road to freedom

Taking the bus in England was not just transport for me. Being able to get
from one place to another without needing anyone is a road to freedom.
When public transport is accessible, a disabled person can take part in
school, work and social life on their own.

## At the airport and on the plane

On my way back to Turkey I also shared the boarding process. That journey
showed me that accessible travel is possible. What matters is the right
support and understanding.

## One ramp, for everyone

A ramp that takes some people a few seconds can be the biggest obstacle of
the day for others. Every ramp built, every space kept clear and every
barrier removed makes life easier not only for wheelchair users, but also
for older people, families with pushchairs and anyone with limited
mobility.

## As a social work student

These experiences showed me once again that accessibility is not a
privilege; it is everyone's right. I also make videos about accessibility
and awareness in Istanbul. In my profession, I want to carry this
perspective and help disabled people live independently.

Awareness grows when it is shared. A barrier-free life is possible,
together.
